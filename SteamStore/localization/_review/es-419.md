# es-419 (Spanish - Latin America) review

Verdict: OK after fixes

Faithful, neutral Latin American Spanish with tú as in the game and no Peninsular forms (no vosotros, coger, a por,
cascos, ratón, ordenador, vídeo): lodo, piso, audífonos, mouse, café, renglón, tarjeta de video, cuenta regresiva, apenas,
ve por la siguiente. Country-bound slang is avoided. Glossary terms match the game (espátula, cepillo, jalador, extractora,
trapeador, entregar, POTENCIA / ÁREA, bono por rapidez / impecable / sequísima / combo, detectores de humedad y de
suciedad). Headers are natural and within 28 characters (Mira cómo regresa el diseño, Agua real, lodo real, A cada
trabajo, su alfombra, Cobra y mejora tu equipo).

Jalador: in running text (sácale el agua sucia con el jalador, empuja el agua sucia fuera de la alfombra con el jalador)
it reads naturally for Mexico, Central America and Peru, the bulk of the Latin American Steam audience, and it is the name
the game already shows. The Southern Cone word (secador) would clash with the game's Secador (Dryer), so jalador is the
right choice; context makes it clear everywhere else.

## Changes
- reveal_body: y el diseño vuelve a aparecer, una pasada satisfactoria a la vez. -> y el diseño vuelve a aparecer con
  cada pasada. Da gusto verlo.
  (fluency: una pasada satisfactoria a la vez is a word-for-word calque of one satisfying pass at a time; the new
  wording keeps both the pass-by-pass reveal and the satisfaction and sounds native)
- rugs_body: sobre una alfombra real de un museo. -> sobre una auténtica alfombra de museo.
  (fluency / ambiguity: alfombra real can be read as royal carpet, and the double indefinite una ... de un museo is
  clumsy; auténtica alfombra de museo says a real museum carpet unambiguously)

## Doubts (left as written)
- short_description: simulador ... que funciona con una simulación de fluidos real is a little heavy, but the source
  itself repeats sim / simulation and the sentence is correct; kept.
- Southern Cone readers (Argentina, Uruguay, Chile) would say auriculares, marrón, barro; audífonos / café / lodo are the
  usual neutral Latin American choices and are understood there.
