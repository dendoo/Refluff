# ms (Malay, Malaysia) review

Verdict: OK as written

Genuine Malaysian Malay, not Indonesian with a few words swapped. It addresses the player as anda, as the game does
(Perkemas peralatan anda), and uses the game's permaidani, not karpet. Glossary terms are used consistently:
Pengikis, Berus (berus berputar), Penyapu air, Penyedut air, Hos, detergen, mop, Serahkan, naik taraf,
KUASA / LIPUTAN in capitals, bonus kelajuan / tanpa cela / kering kontang / kombo, and lampu pemeriksaan (the shop
caption is PEMERIKSAAN). menyerlahkan echoes Serlahan, and "sapu keluar air kotor" is the game's own hint.

Vocabulary checked word by word against Indonesian. Every choice is Malaysian:
- permainan, berasaskan, bendalir, sebenar, habuk, buih, gentian, rawak, degil, muzium, jubin, merah jambu, coklat
- muncung, dilitupi, tiub, laju, mana-mana, lapan, tiada, masa nyata, tetikus, papan kekunci, antara muka
- kad grafik, peranti, serasi, disyorkan, baharu, sistem pengendalian, pemproses, Lesen, fon kepala
- daripada / dari are used correctly: "dirakam daripada mesin sebenar", "dari muzium"
- lists have no comma before dan, as Malay usage requires

No Indonesian words were found: none of busa, serat, uang, acak, ubin, antarmuka, kartu, perangkat, kompatibel or
disarankan appears.

The headers are natural and within 28 characters: Lihat corak muncul semula, Air sebenar, lumpur sebenar,
Lain kerja, lain permaidani, Dapat upah, naik taraf alat. Lain kerja, lain permaidani follows the native "lain X, lain Y"
pattern, as in "lain padang, lain belalang". It carries the note's meaning, that every order brings a different rug.

## Changes
None.

## Doubts (left as written)
- short_description: in "Kikis kerak kering, basahkannya", the -nya could grammatically point back to the crust
  instead of the rug. In context every reader takes it as the rug. The clearer "basahkan permaidani" would push the
  text past the 300-character limit (296 now).
- headphones_body: bulu berus menggeser gentian. Kamus Dewan gives menggeser = menggesel (rub), so it is correct, but
  menggesel would rule out the Indonesian sense "to shift". This is minor and was kept.
- headphones_h2 / sysreq: fon kepala is the standard term (DBP). Many Malaysian gamers simply say headphone, but the
  standard form suits a store page.
