# nl (Dutch) review

Verdict: OK after fixes

## Changes
- hdr_reveal: `Zie het patroon terugkomen` -> `Het patroon duikt weer op` (the "Zie + infinitive" imperative is a calque of "Watch ... come back" and sounds bookish in Dutch; "duikt weer op" = surfaces again, which also fits the pattern emerging from the murky slurry; 25 chars)
- water_body: `schuim stapelt zich op` -> `schuim hoopt zich op` (foam heaps up, it does not stack; "zich ophopen" is the natural collocation)
- alt_clip3_tools: `daarna wordt roze schuim opgespoten` -> `daarna wordt er roze schuim op gespoten` ("opspuiten" reads as raising land or injecting fillers; "er ... op gespoten" = sprayed onto it, as in alt_clip2_water)

## Judged and kept
- short_description "cash je loon" and hdr_paid "Cashen en upgraden": natural informal Dutch, not an awkward anglicism. "Cashen" is an established informal verb in both NL and Flanders (Van Dale: geld opstrijken / incasseren; "prijzengeld cashen", "flink cashen"), gamers and ads use it, and the register matches the game's own shop title "Pimp je gereedschap". "Loon" matches the game (loonstrookje, Basisloon). As a header, two verbs in the infinitive ("Cashen en upgraden") is ordinary Dutch header style. A more neutral option would be "strijk je loon op", but it does not fit the 300-character limit (296 now) and is not better enough to justify cutting elsewhere.
- Other headers: Echt water, echte modder / Elke klus een nieuw tapijt: natural.
- Terms match the glossary: schraper, borstel, trekker, natzuiger, reiniger, dweil, afleveren (lever ... af), snelheidsbonus, vlekkeloos, kurkdroog, combo, KRACHT, BEREIK, haal (the BEREIK legend's word for a pass), je-form.
- System requirements use Steam's Dutch wording (Vereist een 64-bits processor en besturingssysteem, of nieuwer, of beter).

## Doubts (not changed)
- headphones_body "ratelt precies als hij een kruimel opslokt": "precies als" can momentarily read as "just like"; "precies wanneer" would be unambiguous, but in context the temporal reading is clear.
- "elk gereedschap" (paid_body) treats the collective noun as countable; common in speech, "elke tool" would also work.
